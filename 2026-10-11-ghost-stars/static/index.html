Ghost Stars the ratings Google's deleted reviews leave behind

Compare Google Maps ratings side by side: the rating Google shows, the rating with reviews removed after defamation complaints put back in, and the rating of the most recent months.

Proof of concept. Runs on your machine, reads the public Google Maps page with a local browser, and needs no API key. Results are estimates; see the explanations below the table.
Pick an area and a category, then press “Analyse”. Each place takes 10–30 seconds the first time; cached places appear instantly.

What the columns mean

★ / ratings: the average and count from Google's star histogram (more precise than the rounded number in the header).

removed: Google's notice “X to Y reviews removed due to defamation complaints”, shown on business profiles in Germany since April 2026. It counts removals processed in the last 365 days; the removed reviews themselves may be older. The percentage is the share of removed reviews among visible ratings plus removed reviews.

lifetime (scenario): (★ × ratings + s × removed) / (ratings + removed), where s is the star value you chose for a removed review. This is what the rating would be if the removed reviews had that value, not a corrected or recovered rating. Google gives a range, so you see both ends.

12 mo. exp. EXPERIMENTAL scenario: the removals Google processed in the last 365 days are added to the visible reviews posted in the last 12 months. These are not the same cohort: a removal processed this year can concern an older review. The tool also loads at most 150 visible reviews, so for busy places the 12-month count is extrapolated (marked ~). Treat it as a rough indication, not a comparison of like with like.

1 · 2 · 3 · 6 · 12: the average of the newest visible reviews in that window, with the number of reviews n. Hover for a ~95 % range.

Trend (descriptive, sample-limited): average stars per month over the last 12 months, from the loaded reviews only, oldest on the left; months without reviews are skipped. Bigger, darker dots = more reviews that month. Popular places get so many reviews that the loaded ones only cover a few weeks; they are shown per week for the last four weeks instead, because Google switches from “3 weeks ago” to “a month ago” after that (the label says which). The arrow is the change of a line fitted through those averages, weighted by the number of reviews in each period, across the period shown. Less than ±0.2 ★ counts as flat; fewer than 3 periods or 8 reviews shows “too few”.

Sorting: click any column header; click again to reverse. The default is the Google rating, highest first.

Colour: small change · ≥ 0.15 ★ lower · ≥ 0.5 ★ lower. “lifetime” is compared with Google's ★, “12 mo.” with the visible 12-month average.

Why some values are marked

estimated Google's notice uses ranges (“2 to 5”, “201 to 250”) but the top bucket is open-ended: “more than 250”. There is no upper end, so the tool estimates one (the larger of 500, 5 % of the ratings, or twice the lower end). Values without the tag use Google's own range.

~ before a 12-month value: the tool could not load a full year of reviews (very popular places get hundreds a month), so the number of reviews in the last 12 months is extrapolated from the review rate it did see.

Italic window values: the loaded reviews do not reach back that far, so the window only covers part of the period. The row shows how many days back the loaded reviews go.

Dates are approximate because Google only shows relative dates (“2 months ago”).

Cache

Reading a place from Google Maps takes time, so results are stored on your computer (cache/places.json next to the app) for 24 hours. Searching the same area again shows those places instantly; such rows are marked cached.

Tick refresh to ignore the cache and read every place again. Changing the star assumption never needs a refresh: it is only maths on the stored data. Delete cache/places.json to clear everything.

Read this before drawing conclusions

Counting removed reviews as 1 ★ is the minimum-star scenario; 3 ★ is the midpoint scenario. Neither estimates the real, unknown star values of the removed reviews. Some reviews are removed for good reasons, for example fake reviews or insults.

The tool reads business profiles in Germany through the German-language Maps interface, which is where it can find the notice. It only sees the places the bounded Maps search for the area returns and loads, and its page selectors can break when Google changes the page. It is a proof of concept for personal, low-volume use and is not affiliated with Google.
