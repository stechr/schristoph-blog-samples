# Personal Brand Canvas — Nadia

> ⚠️ **Fictional persona** — created to demonstrate the Personal Brand Canvas skill. Not a real person; all details invented. Written to the "what good looks like" bar (see `references/block-guidance.md`): every entry specific, complete, and distinctive.

**Snapshot:** 22, BSc Business Management (first in her family to finish university). Programs Coordinator at a small charity closing the digital-skills gap for young people not in education or employment. Fascinated by how AI is reshaping early-career pathways.

## 🎯 Positioning statement
> I help **young people entering the workforce without a network — and the youth-workers who support them** — to **navigate an AI-shaped job market with clear, practical steps** by **translating the future-of-work conversation into what actually matters for a first job**, because **opportunity shouldn't depend on where you started**.

**Tagline options:**
1. "AI, entry-level, and no hype."
2. "The future of work, translated for the people just starting it."
3. "Your first job in an AI world — decoded."

---

## The Canvas

| Block | Content |
|-------|---------|
| **1 · Purpose & Values** | So that no young person is locked out of the AI economy for lack of a network or someone to explain how it works. Non-negotiables: **access** (opportunity shouldn't depend on your postcode), **honesty** (no hype, ever), **momentum** (one real step beats a five-year plan). |
| **2 · Vision & Goals** | **12 months:** a recognised early voice on AI + first jobs — publishing weekly, one workshop series shipped. **3 years:** the trusted translator between alarming AI-and-jobs headlines and what a 19-year-old should actually do on Monday; advising youth programmes on AI-ready pathways. |
| **3 · Audience** | 17–24-year-olds stepping into work without a professional network, **and** the youth-workers, teachers and programme designers who guide them. *Not for* senior AI strategists or executives. |
| **4 · Unique Value** | The rare three-way overlap: in the room with 19-year-olds every week (frontline), able to read a business case (BSc), and genuinely fluent in how AI is reshaping entry-level work. Most "future of work" voices have at most one of the three. |
| **5 · Strengths & Superpowers** | Turning intimidating jargon into do-it-Monday steps; designing a workshop a nervous teenager actually enjoys; and calling which "AI will take your job" headlines are noise and which ones matter. |
| **6 · Passions & Interests** | One question won't leave me: *what's a genuinely good first job in 2030?* I get angry at doom-scrolling that paralyses young people, and hopeful about AI as a leveller — *if* access is fixed. |
| **7 · Story & Proof** | First in my family to finish university; a placement year showed me how much a foot-in-the-door comes down to luck. Now I run my charity's digital-skills programme — including a 6-week pilot that placed its first cohort, plus an "AI for job-hunting" clinic I built after teaching myself the tools. |
| **8 · Personality & Voice** | Warm, straight-talking, hopeful, a little irreverent. Plain English, zero jargon, always practical — the friend who explains it without making you feel behind. |
| **9 · Channels & Content Pillars** | LinkedIn (primary) + short-form video, occasional newsletter. Pillars: **AI + your first job** (practical decoders), **access & the skills gap** (the frontline view), **field notes** (what I'm learning running programmes). |

## 📌 Content pillars (with example angles)

1. **AI + your first job** — "3 AI skills that actually help you land an entry-level role"; "How to use AI in an application without sounding like a robot."
2. **Access & the skills gap** — "The network you don't have (and how to build one from zero)"; "Why 'just learn to code' is the wrong advice for 2030."
3. **Field notes** — "What 6 weeks of running a digital-skills pilot taught me"; "The one question every teenager asks me about AI."

1. Publish "3 AI skills that actually help you land your first job" on LinkedIn **this week**.
2. Turn the 6-week pilot into a short, honest case-study post (what worked, what didn't).
3. Commit to a weekly cadence: one practical post + one field note.
